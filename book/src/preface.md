## Who this book is for

For programmers who already know a language and want tiko. It does not assume you know BASIC,
and it does not assume you have met FreeBASIC. Where tiko differs from FreeBASIC in a way that
would surprise someone who knows it, a sidebar says so.

## The version this book describes

Every program in this book was compiled and run, on Windows, Linux and macOS wherever it applies, with one pinned
version of the compiler. The pin, and the exact toolchains, are recorded in the book's
repository.

## The reference: the tiko wiki

This book teaches the language; it does not list every keyword, option and library function. That
is the job of the **tiko wiki**,
[github.com/PaulSquires/tiko-lang/wiki](https://github.com/PaulSquires/tiko-lang/wiki), the
language's reference documentation. Keep it open beside the book. The pages you will use most:

- [**Feature Index**](https://github.com/PaulSquires/tiko-lang/wiki/Feature-Index) — every
  language feature and library, one line each, with a link to its reference page.
- [**Function Finder**](https://github.com/PaulSquires/tiko-lang/wiki/Function-Finder) — every
  function, grouped by what you want to do, with one line on what each one does.
- [**Keyword Index**](https://github.com/PaulSquires/tiko-lang/wiki/Keyword-Index) — every
  keyword, statement and operator, A to Z, each with its full definition.
- [**Compatibility**](https://github.com/PaulSquires/tiko-lang/wiki/Compatibility) — for code
  brought from FreeBASIC: what differs, and what is the same.

The wiki also carries tutorials (concurrency, packages and modules, translating C headers) and a
page for each part of the toolchain. Where a chapter shows one form of a statement or a few
members of a library, the wiki has the rest.
